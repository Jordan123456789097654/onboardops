# OnboardOps Data Protection and Privacy Handbook

Owner: Jordan Daniels. This is an operating guide, not legal advice. Have a lawyer review it, and ask one whenever a question touches a law, a regulator or a dispute.

## 1. Whose data is it?
- **Customer data** (a workspace's employees, training records, policies, checklists, case notes) belongs to the customer. We process it on their behalf under our terms and data agreement.
- **Our own data** (account sign-in details, billing, usage, our website visitors) we control and are responsible for directly.
Knowing which kind you are touching decides what you may do with it.

## 2. What we hold
| Group | Examples | Sensitivity |
|---|---|---|
| Account | Name, email, password hash, two-factor and passkey data | High |
| Workspace content | Courses, policies, checklists, quiz results, credentials, notes | Medium to high |
| People records | Employee names, roles, schedules, certifications, incident or discipline notes | High |
| Files | Uploaded certificates and documents | Medium to high |
| Operational | Audit logs, email logs, error logs | Medium |
| Marketing | Prospect names and emails for outreach | Low to medium |

## 3. Rules for everyone
1. **Collect and keep only what we need.**
2. **Use data only for the reason it was given.**
3. **Never move customer data to a personal device, personal cloud, chat tool, or an AI service that is not approved.**
4. **Never put personal data in tickets, screenshots or messages unless essential, and then the minimum.**
5. **Never sell customer data. Never use it for our own marketing.**
6. Treat employee discipline and credential data as especially private.

## 4. AI features
When a feature sends text to an AI provider, the person is told first, only the minimum text is sent, names and contact details are masked where possible, nothing is kept by us in logs beyond counts, and AI output is always a draft a person checks. Features that read people-related text are switched on only after notice to customers under our data agreement. Never paste customer data into any other AI tool.

## 5. Subprocessors
We use outside providers for hosting, database, email, monitoring and AI. Keep an up-to-date list on the website. **Before adding a new provider that handles customer personal data, give customers notice (our agreement sets a window) and update the policy, the data agreement and the security page first.** Check a provider's own terms before stating anything about how they keep data.

## 6. Retention and deletion
- Customers can export and delete their workspace data. After a workspace is closed, we delete its data after a short grace period, then from backups as they age out.
- Audit and security logs are kept long enough to investigate problems and meet legal duties, then removed.
- Outreach contacts: delete or suppress on request, and keep a "do not contact" list so we never email them again.
- Keep a record of what we deleted, when and why, without keeping the data itself.

## 7. Requests from individuals
People may ask to see, correct, export or delete their data, or object to how it is used.
1. Log the request and its date.
2. **Verify identity strongly** (Support Handbook section 4).
3. Work out who controls the data. For workspace data, usually the customer (the employer), so direct the person to their employer and tell the customer if our agreement requires it.
4. Reply within the deadline (commonly 30 days; shorter in some places). Ask Jordan before sending anything in a legal context.
5. Record the outcome.

## 8. Requests from authorities, lawyers or others
Never hand over data because someone says they are entitled. **Stop and ask Jordan.** Ask for the request in writing, check it is valid, tell the customer unless the law forbids it, and give the minimum required.

## 9. Marketing and outreach
- Business contacts only, from public sources, with an honest reason for contacting them.
- Every message says who we are, why we are writing and how to opt out. Honour opt-outs immediately and permanently.
- Never buy lists, never scrape personal accounts, never use deceptive subjects.
- Outreach follows the laws that apply to commercial email (for example the CAN-SPAM Act in the US). Keep sending authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) healthy so our product mail is not harmed.

## 10. Security of data
Follow the Security and Access Handbook. Customer integration keys are encrypted; limited staff access; data encrypted in transit and at rest by our providers; test environments never use real customer data.

## 11. Breaches
Follow the Incident Response Handbook (7.4). Contact a lawyer promptly; breach notice deadlines can be as short as 72 hours and vary by place and contract.

## 12. Records we keep
- List of subprocessors and what each does.
- Record of requests and how we answered.
- Record of incidents and reviews.
- Record of staff training and access reviews.
- Current privacy policy, terms, data agreement and employee privacy notice, with their change dates.
