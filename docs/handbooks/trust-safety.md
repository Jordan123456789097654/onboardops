# OnboardOps Trust and Safety Handbook

Owner: Alan (COO). Backup: Jordan Daniels. Covers abuse reports, moderation, bans, workspace suspensions and appeals.

## 1. Purpose
Keep the platform safe and lawful for customers and their employees, treat everyone fairly, and leave a clear record of every decision.

## 2. What we act on
| Category | Examples | Usual first action |
|---|---|---|
| Security abuse | Attacks, scraping, credential stuffing, spamming invites | Contain, rate-limit or suspend, then incident process |
| Fraud | Fake accounts, stolen payment, impersonating a company | Suspend, verify, report to the payment provider |
| Harassment and threats | Abusive text in training, notes or messages | Remove content, warn or ban, escalate threats to Jordan at once |
| Illegal content | Anything unlawful or exploiting people | Preserve evidence, remove, get legal advice, report where required |
| Misuse | Using the product for something it is not meant for, mass unsolicited email | Warn, then restrict |
| Terms or non-payment | Repeated breach, unpaid after notice | Notice, then suspend |

## 3. Principles
1. **Proportionate.** Start with the mildest action that stops the harm.
2. **Evidence first.** Record what we saw, when, and from where, before acting. Preserve it.
3. **Two sets of eyes** for permanent actions where possible.
4. **Explain.** Tell the person which rule applied and how to appeal.
5. **No surprises, no favourites.** Same facts, same outcome.
6. **People first when safety is at risk.** Threats of harm go to Jordan immediately; consider emergency services.

## 4. The process
1. **Receive** the report. Acknowledge it. Never promise an outcome.
2. **Preserve** evidence (screens, audit entries, times, account and workspace names). Keep it in the case record, not in chat.
3. **Assess** severity and whether this is an incident (Incident Response Handbook 7.8).
4. **Decide** using the ladder: warning, remove content, temporary restriction, workspace suspension, account ban.
5. **Act** with the moderation tools and write the reason in the case record.
6. **Notify** the person and, when relevant, the workspace owner. Do not reveal who reported.
7. **Review** later: did it work, does the rule need clarifying?

## 5. Workspace suspension
Suspend when there is a serious or continuing breach or a security risk. Tell the owner what happened, what they can do, and how to appeal. Keep customer data intact and protected while suspended. Lift it once the cause is resolved and record why.

## 6. Account bans
A banned account cannot sign in or keep a session. Use for serious or repeated abuse. Record the reason and the evidence. Where a workspace owner is banned, tell the other owners or admins so the business is not stranded, and discuss handover with Jordan.

## 7. Appeals
- Anyone may appeal. Accept appeals by email.
- A different person from the one who decided reviews it, with fresh eyes.
- Reply within 5 working days with the decision and the reason.
- If we were wrong, reverse the action, apologise and fix the record.
- Keep appeal outcomes to improve the rules.

## 8. Reports about staff or our own conduct
Anyone can report a concern about us. Take it seriously, thank them, and have the person not involved review it. Keep it confidential and non-retaliatory.

## 9. Requests from authorities
Do not hand over data on request. Follow the Data Protection Handbook section 8: stop, ask Jordan, ask for a written valid request, tell the customer unless forbidden, share the minimum.

## 10. Records
Each case has: ID, date, who reported, what was reported, evidence, severity, decision, who decided, action, notice sent, appeal and outcome. Review monthly for patterns, and publish clear rules where confusion arises.
